Sound Familiar?

"Should we rebuild this thing?"

Every feature takes longer than it did a year ago. New hires wince at the codebase. The team wants to rebuild it in a new framework. Six months, tops.

→ Usually not. Most rewrites take two to three times longer than estimated and ship with the same problems, because the problem was never the framework. The real choice is go, no-go, or replace it piece by piece. Rebuild your most painful workflow in two weeks first and see if it's actually better. I've watched $50M rewrites never ship.

"We're shipping fast with AI. Is it a house of cards?"

The team is smaller. Output is up. But you don't really know what the AI can touch: customer data, billing, live systems. Or what breaks when traffic triples. You'll find out when a customer does.

→ Use AI hard. Treat it like a talented junior dev: scoped tasks, review everything, never let it make architecture decisions. Keep people who understand your system making the calls that matter. I'll tell you which shortcuts are fine and which ones will cost you six months.

"Is this hire actually senior?"

You're on your fifth senior-dev interview this month. Or you're a non-technical founder and can't tell a solid engineer from someone who interviews well.

→ Most hiring mistakes happen before the interview. Do you need a full-time hire or a three-month project? What does success look like at 90 days? A second opinion before the offer is the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy.

"I'm presenting architecture to a client Friday."

You promised them a scale you've never built. If you're wrong, you spend six months fixing it. Or lose the client. Maybe both.

→ Pressure-test it before they see it. Better to hear the hard questions from me than from your client.

"My team is busy, but nothing ships."

A two-week feature turned into two months. A competitor shipped the same thing in three. Standups sound great. When you ask what shipped this month, the room gets quiet.

→ Could be the devs. Could be the process. Could be features that are too big. Usually it's all three. Most messes grow from two or three early decisions. We find those and leave the other 45 items on the list alone.

How I Think

  • Rewrites are bets. Small bets beat big ones.
  • Arguments produce opinions. Experiments produce data. A two-day pilot settles what two weeks of meetings can't.
  • Technical debt is a loan. Take it on purpose, write it down, pay it back before the interest buries you.
  • Your two-person team doesn't need microservices. You need to ship.
  • Motion isn't progress. What shipped?
