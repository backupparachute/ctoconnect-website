Sound Familiar?

"Should we rebuild this thing?"

Every feature takes longer than last year. New hires wince at the codebase. The team wants a rebuild in a new framework. Six months, tops.

→ Usually not. Most rewrites run two to three times over the estimate and ship with the same problems. The framework was never the problem. Your real options are go, no go, or replace it piece by piece. Rebuild your worst workflow in two weeks first and see if it's better. I've watched $50M rewrites never ship.

"We ship fast with AI. Is it a house of cards?"

Your team is smaller. Output is up. You don't know what the AI can touch (customer data, billing) or what breaks when traffic triples. A customer will find out first.

→ Use AI hard. Treat it like a talented junior dev. Give it scoped tasks. Review everything. Never let it make architecture decisions. Keep people who know your system on the calls that matter. I'll tell you which shortcuts are fine and which ones cost you six months.

"Is this candidate senior, or good at interviews?"

Fifth senior dev interview this month. You still can't tell who will ship.

→ Most hiring mistakes happen before the interview. Do you need a full time hire or a three month project? What does success look like at 90 days? A second opinion before the offer is cheap insurance.

"I'm presenting architecture to a client Friday."

You promised a scale you've never built. If you're wrong, you spend six months fixing it. Or you lose the client.

→ Pressure test it first. Hear the hard questions from me before your client asks them.

"My team is busy. Nothing ships."

A two week feature took two months. A competitor shipped it in three weeks. Standups sound great. Ask what shipped this month and the room goes quiet.

→ Could be the devs. Could be the process. Usually both. Most messes grow from two or three early decisions. We find those and ignore the rest of the list.
