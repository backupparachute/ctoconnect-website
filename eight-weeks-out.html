Something Is Coming

A grant deadline. A pitch competition. A raise. A customer who wants a real demo and a straight answer about your roadmap.

Right now your operation is you, remembering things.

You don't have a process, you have habits. Work happens when you get to it. You can't say how long a change takes, because it depends. If someone asked how you decide what to build next, you would invent an answer on the spot.

That holds up fine until the day somebody with money asks.

What Eight Weeks Does

You ship on a cadence you can point at. You know your numbers because you measured them. And when someone asks how you build, you have an answer ready instead of a story.

Eight weeks gets your side of it into shape. Whether you win the competition or land the round depends on your market and a room of people neither of us controls.

Eight weeks. Six of them building.

The Numbers

Measured in week one. Measured again in week eight. On a board the whole cohort can see.

  • Commit to production. How long from writing a line of code to that code being live.
  • Deploy count. How many times you shipped in the last 30 days.
  • Time to turn it off. How long it takes to kill a broken change.
  • Your top metric. The one number that matters, and what it says today.

Sitting next to five other founders watching the same board is most of why people finish.

Week One Is Yours

Ninety minutes, just you and me

Inside your codebase. Your pipeline, your config, your branching, your secrets, who else can deploy.

Then you get a written page. The three things most likely to hurt you, and the one to fix first.

That lands before you have watched a single lesson.

How A Week Runs

Monday is the clinic

Ninety minutes, everyone on. We look at the board, work through whatever got people stuck, then put two founders' real problems on screen.

Tuesday and Thursday

Two lessons, ten minutes each, each with something to try. Then you build.

Wednesday

Office hours. Drop in if you are stuck.

Friday

Post your ship note. What you shipped, how many times you deployed, the one thing next, where you got stuck, and anything that felt good. Four minutes.

Weekends

Nothing. Not from you, not from me.

The Six Build Weeks

Two · Push and it goes live

An automated pipeline, with nothing between your keyboard and production.

Three · Ship it turned off

Risky changes go out behind a switch. Something breaks, you flip it off in seconds, and your cofounder can do it without knowing how any of this works.

Four · See what people actually do

Events and a funnel, so there is one number worth watching.

Five · Cut the time from idea to live

Measure it honestly. Then halve it. This is the week the board starts moving for everyone.

Six · Stop arguing and run the test

The same switch from week three, now putting a change in front of 10% of your users so the data settles it.

Seven · Price what you are carrying

Write down the shortcuts, what each one will cost you, and which ones you are keeping on purpose. This becomes a page you can hand to somebody.

Week Eight Is Demo Week

Two hours, the whole cohort, cameras on. You show the four numbers from week one against where they sit now, what you shipped, and what one experiment taught you about your users.

Nobody presents a product demo. The product was there in week one. What you present is the change.

What You Walk Away Holding

  • A pipeline that deploys without you touching it
  • A kill switch you have tested, so a broken feature disappears in under a minute
  • Eight weeks of real shipping data, with a start and an end
  • A written page on your technical debt that you can hand to an investor
  • Answers. Every technical question a judge or an investor asks, with the artifacts to back them up
  • Five other founders who know your codebase

This is for you if

  • You have a live app with real users, even a handful
  • You deploy by hand, or you have automation you do not trust
  • Something is coming in the next year and you would rather not improvise when it arrives

This is not

  • For anyone who has not built anything yet
  • For teams already shipping several times a day with numbers that look good
  • A course you watch. Your code goes on screen in week one.
